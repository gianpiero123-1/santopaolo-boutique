A long stay is not a longer trip, it is a different thing entirely. The priorities shift: a central position matters less than daily liveability, and practical details weigh far more than they seem to when booking.

## What changes from a short trip

**Shopping becomes daily.** You need a supermarket or market within walking distance every day, not once.

**Noise becomes unbearable.** One night above nightlife is tolerable. Thirty nights is not. This is the main reason the historic centre works poorly for long stays.

**You need laundry.** A washing machine in the apartment or a launderette nearby. Without it, the problem is real within two weeks.

**Internet matters.** If you work, connection speed is a requirement rather than a detail. Ask for the figure, not the word wifi.

## What to check before booking

Heating and air conditioning, because Naples needs both at different times of year. Mid-stay cleaning, included or charged. Linen changes and how often. Utilities, included in the rate or metered separately. The deposit and how it is returned.

And the monthly rate, which is almost always different from the sum of the nights and has to be asked for explicitly.

<InPractice>
- Always request a dedicated quote for stays beyond two weeks
- Check whether parking is available, monthly street parking is unworkable
- Ask whether the property can issue an invoice, useful for work assignments
- Check there is a real desk rather than a dining table
- Ask which way the apartment faces, in summer it changes everything
</InPractice>

## Why Chiaia works for long stays

It is residential, so it has the service network a tourist area lacks: food shops, pharmacies, launderettes, gyms, markets. Evenings have life without constant nightlife.

The sea is close and Lungomare Caracciolo becomes the place you walk or run every day, which over a month matters more than any monument.
