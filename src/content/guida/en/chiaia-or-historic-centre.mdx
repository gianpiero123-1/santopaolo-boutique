These are two different experiences of the same city, thirty minutes apart on foot. Neither is objectively better, but choosing one without knowing what it involves can spoil a trip.

## The historic centre

**In favour.** You are inside postcard Naples. Spaccanapoli, San Gregorio Armeno, Cappella Sansevero, the long-standing pizzerias, all within a ten minute walk. The density of things to see is the highest in the city.

**Against.** Noise. The narrow lanes amplify everything and nightlife runs late for most of the year. Apartments are often carved out of old buildings with poor sound insulation and limited light.

Light sleepers, families with small children and anyone working early usually struggle here.

## Chiaia

**In favour.** Residential, wider streets, nineteenth century buildings with high ceilings. The sea is close, the shops are the best in the city, and evenings feel like ordinary life.

**Against.** It costs more, eating and drinking included. And it is not characterful: anyone chasing the classic image of Naples has to travel for it.

## The actual distances

From Vico Santa Maria a Cappella Vecchia: Spaccanapoli 1.8 km, Cappella Sansevero 2 km, San Gregorio Armeno 2.2 km, the Archaeological Museum 2.2 km. That is thirty minutes on foot, or a few minutes by metro.

In the other direction: Piazza dei Martiri 70 metres, Teatro San Carlo 610 metres, Castel dell'Ovo 1 km, Lungomare Caracciolo 1.1 km.

<InPractice>
- First visit, three days, monuments as the priority: historic centre
- With children, in a group, or staying more than four nights: Chiaia
- If noise wrecks your sleep, Chiaia without hesitation
- If the evening budget is tight, the historic centre is cheaper for dinner
- Arriving by car, Chiaia offers more workable parking options than the lanes
</InPractice>

## A practical rule

The historic centre is for visiting, Chiaia is for staying. People coming to see things choose the first, people coming to be somewhere choose the second.
