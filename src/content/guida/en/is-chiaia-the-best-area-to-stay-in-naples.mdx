Chiaia sits between the sea and the hill, west of Piazza del Plebiscito. It is where Neapolitans shop, take their children to school and go out for dinner, and it holds the highest concentration of shops in the city.

## What sets it apart

**It is residential, not touristic.** Evenings feel like ordinary city life rather than queues outside pizzerias. Anyone looking for the constant noise of the historic centre will not find it here, which is precisely why many people choose it.

**Everything is walkable.** From Vico Santa Maria a Cappella Vecchia, Piazza dei Martiri is 70 metres away, Teatro San Carlo 610 metres, the Bourbon Gallery 350 metres, Castel dell'Ovo one kilometre. Lungomare Caracciolo takes around fifteen minutes on foot.

**The historic centre stays close.** Spaccanapoli is 1.8 km away, Cappella Sansevero 2 km, San Gregorio Armeno 2.2 km. That is a thirty minute walk or a few minutes by metro.

## The honest trade-offs

Chiaia is not cheap. Restaurants and bars are priced for an affluent neighbourhood, and that shows up in daily spending.

It is also not the most characteristic part of the city. Visitors arriving in search of narrow lanes and washing strung between balconies will need to travel to find them, because Chiaia is nineteenth century and its streets are wider.

Traffic at peak hours is heavy and the inner streets are narrow, so arriving by car without a fixed destination makes the day harder than it needs to be.

- Travelling as a group, check the rooms are in one building rather than scattered across the area
- Verify walking distance to the seafront rather than straight line distance

## Who it works best for

Families, groups, longer stays and repeat visitors. Anyone with three days who only wants the historic centre is probably better placed further east.
