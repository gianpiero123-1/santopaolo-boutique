The number platforms display largest is maximum guests. It is also the least useful number, because it adds up different things.

## What stated capacity hides

**Sofa beds.** An apartment for six often means two bedrooms and a sofa bed in the living room. Whoever sleeps there has no door, no wardrobe, and goes to bed when everyone else stops standing up.

That is not a flaw in itself, but it needs knowing before you divide rooms among five people.

**Bathrooms.** This is the real bottleneck. Six people with one bathroom means an hour of queueing every morning. The right question is not how many beds, it is how many bathrooms.

**Bed sizes.** A double counts as two people, but the width varies considerably between apartments.

## The questions to ask

How many bedrooms have a door. How many beds are sofa beds. How many bathrooms there are and whether they have a shower or a bath. Whether single beds can be joined.

For mixed groups of couples and singles, distribution matters more than total capacity.

<InPractice>
- Count bathrooms before beds, it is the number that shapes the day
- Ask which sleeping places are in bedrooms and which in shared areas
- For groups, check whether several units let each couple have their own room
- Ask whether linen is provided for the full stated capacity
- Check the charge for extra guests above a given threshold
</InPractice>

## How the five Chiaia units are laid out

On Vico Santa Maria a Cappella Vecchia the units differ in layout, which is exactly why a group can combine them to suit itself.

The 90 square metre unit has two bedrooms and two bathrooms, sleeping up to six. The 70 and one of the 60 square metre units have a bedroom plus sofa bed, sleeping up to four. A second 60 square metre unit is a suite for two with an en suite bathroom. The smallest, at 45 square metres, is a studio for two.

Booking more than one, a group of ten or twelve gets more enclosed bedrooms and more bathrooms than any single apartment of the same capacity, while staying on one floor.
