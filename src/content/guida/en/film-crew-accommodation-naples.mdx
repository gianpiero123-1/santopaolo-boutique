Anyone running a production in Naples knows the problem: the city is an extraordinary location and a difficult logistics base. The centre is dense, the streets are narrow, and accommodation is not built for people arriving with vans and cases.

## The three requirements that must coincide

**Beds close together.** A crew spread across three hotels loses time every morning and every evening. What is needed is units in the same building, ideally on the same level.

**Vehicles secured.** Production vans cannot sit on the street overnight, for insurance reasons before practical ones. And central public garages carry height limits that exclude most of the fleet.

**Equipment protected.** Cameras, lenses, lighting, monitors. They come out and go back every day, and they need an enclosed space.

When one of the three is missing, the production pays for it in lost time, which in Naples means crossing the traffic twice a day.

## What to ask a property

It is not about stars. It is these questions.

How many units are in the same building and across how many floors. What is the maximum height at the garage entrance. Whether the garage is covered and enclosed or merely marked out. Whether there is space to leave material beyond the vehicles. Whether check in is flexible, because crews never arrive at standard hours. Whether the property can invoice the production company.

<InPractice>
- Verify the entrance height before signing, not on arrival day
- Ask whether access is available overnight for late returns
- For stays over a week, request a whole-property rate
- Check laundry availability, on long shoots it matters
- Agree loading and unloading arrangements in advance for narrow streets
</InPractice>

## The Chiaia case

Vico Santa Maria a Cappella Vecchia 8b brings the three conditions together at one address: five apartments on a single floor with up to eighteen beds, a 3,000 square metre covered on-site garage with a 3 metre height limit and access 24 hours a day every day, an electric charging point, and a Chiaia position a short distance from the seafront and the centre.

The 3 metre clearance is the figure that matters to a production: vehicles with roof racks, antennas or internal rigging get in without stripping anything down.

For production enquiries, booking direct is the route, since it allows the whole property to be held and access and invoicing agreed in a single conversation.
