Naples Capodichino sits unusually close to the city, roughly 6.8 kilometres from Chiaia. That is an advantage, though urban traffic gives back some of the distance saved.

## Taxi

The most direct option and the one that makes sense with luggage. Naples applies fixed predetermined fares between the airport and the main city destinations, so the price does not move with traffic.

Agree the fixed fare before setting off and state your exact destination. If the meter is started without you having asked for the fixed rate, the final amount can differ.

Real journey time: twenty minutes in good conditions, thirty or more at peak hours and on Friday afternoons.

## Alibus

The dedicated public link between the airport and the city, stopping in the centre and at the port. It is inexpensive and reliable on timing, but it does not reach Chiaia, so you will need a short taxi ride or walk at the end.

It makes sense if you travel light, are on your own, or arrive at an hour when traffic slows a taxi down anyway.

## Private transfer

Booked in advance, with a driver waiting on arrival. It costs more than a taxi but removes the taxi rank queue, which can be long during the summer peak.

Worth it for groups, late night arrivals, and anyone travelling with children or several bags.

<InPractice>
- Groups over four need two taxis or a van, confirm before arrival
- Flights landing after 23:00 find fewer taxis available, book ahead
- If your accommodation has a garage, telling them your arrival time lets them coordinate access
- With a rental car, remember central Naples has restricted traffic zones and difficult street parking
- Fixed fares change by municipal order, confirm the amount with the driver
</InPractice>

## Arriving by car

Capodichino connects to Chiaia via the ring road with an exit towards the centre. The final stretch runs through narrow urban streets, so allow at least thirty minutes and make sure you have a definite place to leave the vehicle.
