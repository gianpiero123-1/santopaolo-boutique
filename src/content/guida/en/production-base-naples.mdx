A production shooting in Naples often ends up with three bases: a hotel for people, a car park for vehicles and a warehouse for kit. A proper production base turns that into one. In Chiaia, at Vico Santa Maria a Cappella Vecchia 8b, the same building has 20 beds, a 3,000 sq m covered garage with 100 spaces and more than 1,000 sq m of open floor.

## What is a production base?

A production base is the fixed point a shoot leaves from each morning and comes back to each night, where the vehicles, the kit and ideally the people all stay.

Departments head out to set from the base and everything returns there at wrap. The tighter the base, the fewer internal transfers between hotel, car park and store.

## Why is it hard to find one in central Naples?

Large covered spaces that vehicles can actually reach are rare in central Naples, because the city is built on historic buildings and narrow lanes.

Public garages often have height limits that rule out tall vans. Stopping in the street to load means blocking the road or applying for a street occupation permit, with the lead time that comes with it. That is why so many productions keep their kit on the outskirts and their crew in the centre.

## What should a base for a shoot have?

A base for a shoot needs covered vehicle access, sheltered loading and unloading, round the clock supervision, a lockable store and accommodation with zero travel time.

Two further details only show up in person: the real height of the entrance and the room to manoeuvre. A big garage with a tight ramp is no use to a production van.

## How much space does the Chiaia base have?

The building at Vico Santa Maria a Cappella Vecchia 8b has a 3,000 sq m covered garage: 100 parking spaces and, inside it, more than 1,000 sq m of open floor assigned to the production.

The open floor is allocated by the square metre for the length of the shoot and laid out around what that production needs. The same building holds five apartments on a single floor, 20 beds in total.

## Which vehicles fit in the garage?

Anything up to 3 metres high fits: production vans, fitted vans, SUVs, 9 seat minibuses, generators and wheeled lighting units.

A box truck over 3 metres will not clear the entrance. In that case the base still works for people, cars and kit, and the tall vehicle is handled separately. Check this with real measurements, not the rental company's spec sheet.

## Does it avoid street occupation permits?

Yes, for loading and unloading at base: vehicles drive in and work inside the building rather than standing in the street.

Everything else stays the same. Exterior shooting still needs its own permits from the council, and the base does not replace them. What it removes is the daily problem of a van parked in a Chiaia side street at seven in the morning.

## Who handles access and deliveries?

Access is open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, with full CCTV, and on-site staff sign for deliveries.

A rental drop that arrives while the crew is on set does not sit outside the gate. It comes in, goes to the assigned area and is waiting when the crew gets back.
