This is the line that surprises guests most often on arrival, because it is almost never included in the price shown by booking platforms.

## How it works

The tourist tax is a municipal charge applied per person per night to anyone staying in registered accommodation. The property collects it and passes it to the city.

It is not a property mark-up and it is due regardless of which booking channel you used.

## Why it is not in the platform price

Platforms display the accommodation price and their own fees. The tourist tax is collected by the property during the stay, so it sits outside the displayed total.

This is why the final cost can exceed expectations, particularly for large groups and long stays, where the amount multiplies across people and nights.

## Exemptions

Exempt categories are set by municipal regulation and vary between cities. They typically cover children below a certain age, some categories of accompanying adults, and stays beyond a maximum number of consecutive nights.

The qualifying age for children is the variable that differs most between municipalities, so it should be checked rather than assumed.

<InPractice>
- Ask for the per person per night amount before arrival, not at check out
- Check whether payment is cash or card, some properties accept only one
- For groups, calculate the total in advance, across ten people it adds up
- Ask for the receipt, it is issued against payment
- Check the night threshold beyond which long stays become exempt
</InPractice>

## A note for business travellers

The tourist tax follows different rules from the accommodation invoice. If you need documentation for expense reimbursement, ask the property for it explicitly at the time of payment.
