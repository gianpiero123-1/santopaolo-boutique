This is the moment a stay starts well or badly, and it is almost always a matter of communication rather than logistics.

## Why it differs from a hotel

A hotel always has someone there. An apartment does not, so entry requires an arrangement. Anyone arriving without having sent an arrival time ends up on the street with their luggage until they reach somebody.

## The methods

**Meeting in person.** Someone waits, lets you in, explains the place. This is the better version because it answers questions immediately, but it needs an agreed time.

**Access code.** Instructions arrive before you get there and you enter when you like. Convenient for night flights and delays, provided the instructions are clear and someone answers the phone if something fails.

## What to agree before leaving

Your approximate arrival time and how to update it if it changes. What happens if you arrive before the entry time, meaning whether you can drop bags. What happens if you arrive after 20:00. A phone number that genuinely answers.

And the exact address with the drop-off point for a taxi, because narrow Naples side streets are not always drivable.

<InPractice>
- Send your arrival time as soon as you have tickets, not the day before
- If the flight is delayed, say so immediately, an hour's notice changes everything
- Ask whether you can leave luggage before the check in time
- Check whether all guests' documents need sending before arrival
- Save the direct number in your phone, not just in the email
</InPractice>

## At Vico Santa Maria a Cappella Vecchia

Entry is arranged case by case around your arrival time, sent before departure. Guests arriving by car drive straight into the building's garage, so there is no hunting for somewhere to stop and unload.

For groups taking several units, check in happens once rather than five times over.
