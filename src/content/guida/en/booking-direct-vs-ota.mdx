Booking platforms are convenient and solved a real problem: trust between strangers. But they charge a commission that somebody pays, and that somebody is always the guest, either directly or through a higher rate.

## What actually changes

**Price.** Platform commission typically runs between ten and twenty per cent of the booking value. Part of that margin can come back to the guest as a lower direct rate, or become included services.

**Flexibility.** Cancellation terms on platforms are standardised. Direct, they can be agreed, particularly for longer stays or multiple bookings.

**Communication.** On a platform, messages route through the system and answers arrive when they arrive. Direct, you speak to whoever runs the property.

**Unusual requests.** A cot, a late arrival, garage access, adjoining units for a group. These do not fit a platform form and get resolved in one conversation when booking direct.

## What you give up

This deserves saying plainly. The platform provides an intermediary if something goes wrong, a verified review system and a standardised refund process. Booking direct, that safety net is absent.

Which is why direct booking makes sense when the property is identifiable: a visible company name and VAT number, a published national identification code, a real address, a phone that answers.

<InPractice>
- Check the site shows the operating company, VAT number and CIN code
- Ask for written confirmation with dates, rate and terms
- Be wary of anyone asking for the full amount by bank transfer with no contract
- For groups and long stays, always request a direct quote, the gap is wider
- Compare the direct rate against the platform rate on equivalent cancellation terms
</InPractice>

## When it genuinely pays

Stays over three nights, multi-unit bookings, business travel needing an invoice, and anyone with a request that does not fit into a form field.

For a single night in low season the difference is marginal and the platform stays convenient.
