Naples carries a reputation that has outlived the reality by decades and no longer matches what a visitor experiences today. Chiaia in particular is an affluent residential district with neighbourhood life running late.

## What it feels like after dark

The main streets, via dei Mille, via Filangieri, via Santa Caterina a Chiaia, are lit and busy. Piazza dei Martiri and the surrounding streets have venues open late, so there are no deserted stretches during evening hours.

Lungomare Caracciolo is used for walking and running well into the evening for most of the year.

## Sensible precautions

These are the same as any large city, and none are specific to Naples.

Never leave anything visible in a parked car, not even for ten minutes. That holds everywhere in the city and across Italy generally.

Keep an eye on your phone and bag in crowds, particularly in busy tourist areas and on public transport.

Late at night the narrower inner streets are quieter, so the main routes are the better choice.

## How it compares to other areas

Chiaia has neither the chaotic pedestrian congestion of the historic centre nor the isolation of outer districts. It sits in between, with ordinary daily life and a resident population that keeps the streets naturally occupied.

## The short version

The answer is yes. Chiaia is among the areas where a visitor moves around Naples most comfortably, and the useful precautions are the ones you would apply in Rome, Milan or any European city of similar size.
