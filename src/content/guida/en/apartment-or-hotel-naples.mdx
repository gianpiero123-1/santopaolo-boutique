The question resolves on three variables: how many nights, how many people, and whether the trip runs at one pace throughout.

## When a hotel works better

**One or two nights.** There is no time to shop and cook, so the extra space serves no purpose.

**Travelling alone or as a couple.** The economies of an apartment never kick in, and included breakfast carries real value.

**Unpredictable arrival times.** Reception is always there. Apartments solve this with self check in, but it has to be arranged.

**Daily service.** Housekeeping and linen changes are standard in hotels, optional elsewhere.

## When an apartment works better

**Three nights and up.** Breakfast at home and one dinner in three changes the total cost of the stay, and not marginally.

**Groups and families.** Shared space where everyone can be together is the one thing a hotel cannot give. With children, cooking and keeping your own rhythm matters more than any service.

**Long stays and remote work.** A desk, a washing machine and a kitchen make a month sustainable. A hotel room does not.

**Naples specifically.** Shopping in the markets and neighbourhood shops is part of the city, and without a kitchen it stays out of reach.

## The middle ground

Some properties combine both: apartments with hotel-style service, shared facilities and direct assistance from the operator. These answer people who want apartment space without giving up the organisation.

<InPractice>
- Under three nights a hotel is almost always simpler
- Over four people an apartment is almost always cheaper
- Check whether a mid-stay clean is included or charged
- Ask how check in works if you arrive after 20:00
- For business travel, confirm the property can invoice your company
</InPractice>

## The short version

It is not a question of category but of length and headcount. Two nights alone: hotel. Five nights for six people: apartment, without debate.
